# Iteration 12 Report

## What changed
Two candidates targeting the frontier (`adaptive_tokenizer_confusion_memory`, 51.6% avg):

- **anchor_recency_confusion_memory** (exploitation, axis D/C): Replaced the unbounded example
  pool with a bounded structure — one "anchor" example per label (always updated to most recent)
  plus an 80-example recency window — hypothesizing that LawBench's 116-label space needs forced
  per-label coverage and that a bounded pool would avoid dilution from early-training examples.

- **discriminative_similarity_memory** (exploration, axis C/B): Replaced plain Jaccard with
  label-discriminativeness-weighted Jaccard where `w(t) = 1 / num_distinct_labels_containing_t`,
  down-weighting tokens that appear across many labels and up-weighting label-exclusive tokens.

## Results
Both regressed versus the frontier:
- `anchor_recency_confusion_memory`: 45.6% (-6.0)
- `discriminative_similarity_memory`: 47.8% (-3.8)

## Why they regressed

**anchor_recency_confusion_memory**: A hard recency cutoff of 80 destroys coverage for rare labels
that appear once in training and then not again. The anchor dict mitigates this only partially —
anchors get stale as training progresses, and the 80-example window still misses many labels on
tasks with 100+ classes. The net effect was worse coverage than the unbounded pool.

**discriminative_similarity_memory**: Discriminativeness weighting assumes that tokens unique to
one or two labels are the most reliable signal. In practice, such tokens are often noise (rare
proper nouns, OCR artifacts) that happen to appear in one training example. Downweighting common
tokens removed real signal — words like verb roots or domain terms that appear across several
labels are often the most useful retrieval anchors.

## Takeaway for future iterations
- Pool bounding consistently regresses (two attempts now); do not revisit.
- Token-level re-weighting has been tried three ways (IDF, discriminativeness, adaptive script)
  and only script-adaptivity helped. Don't revisit per-token weighting beyond that.
- The two-phase architecture (confusion slots → similarity fill) may be sub-optimal because
  confusion slots can be low-similarity to the query. A **unified single-pass scoring** that
  multiplies similarity by confusion affinity would avoid wasting slots on irrelevant examples.
- Per-label temporal tracking of error rates (with decay) has not been tried and targets a
  different axis from the confusion matrix.
