2	
3	Extends decayed_confusion_memory with a novel prompt-ordering mechanism (axis A):
4	after the standard two-phase retrieval, the selected examples are re-ordered
5	before formatting so that examples whose labels are most frequently the *true*
6	label in confusion-matrix errors appear LAST in the prompt — immediately before
7	the question.
8	
9	Why this might help: transformer attention exhibits a recency bias, meaning
10	positions close to the generation boundary exert disproportionately strong
11	influence on the prediction.  In the standard system, selection order determines
12	prompt order, so the highest-similarity example (which is often a generic
13	representative of a common label) ends up first and the rare disambiguation
14	example ends up last by accident.  By sorting ascending on confusion-target
15	affinity we deliberately push the examples the model most needs for
16	disambiguation to the position where they have maximal influence — right above
17	the question — while leaving easy/unambiguous examples earlier.
18	
19	Sort key: confusion column affinity of the example's label = sum of all
20	confusion counts where that label is the ground-truth (the model predicted
21	something else but this was correct).  High affinity ↔ label is frequently
22	mis-predicted ↔ disambiguation examples for this label are most valuable near
23	the question.
24	
25	Cold start: all affinities are 0, so stable sort preserves the original
26	similarity order exactly — no cold-start regression.
27	
28	Everything else (retrieval, decay, normalisation, adaptive tokenizer) is
29	identical to decayed_confusion_memory.  Any accuracy delta is attributable
30	purely to the ordering change.
