19	## Why confusion_disambig leads overall but regresses on LawBench
20	The disambiguation phase helped Symptom2Disease (many similar symptoms, subtle label boundaries)
21	lift from 88 → 90%. But on LawBench it dropped from 34% (mmr) to 26%. The cause: after the
22	disambiguation phase, the fill is pure similarity rank, which clusters near-duplicate examples
23	and wastes budget. MMR had been suppressing that redundancy. So disambiguation + similarity fill
24	hurts LawBench by displacing the diversity signal that mmr_memory provided.
25	
26	label_aware_prompt (-3.8) regressed because listing all labels in every prompt adds noise and
27	token overhead that crowds out useful examples, especially when the label list is long. The model
28	appears to free-associate from the label list rather than from the few-shot examples.
29	
30	## Takeaway for future iterations
31	1. The disambiguation phase is worth keeping — it wins on Symptom2Disease. The fill phase is the
32	   weakness. Replace the similarity-rank fill with an MMR fill to recover LawBench performance.
33	2. LLM synthesis in learning (axis F, reflexion and now implicitly label_aware) keeps regressing.
34	   Low-cost learning signals (confusion matrix, per-label error rates) are safer.
35	3. LawBench diversity signal (MMR) and Symptom2Disease confusion signal are complementary, not
36	   mutually exclusive. A hybrid should beat both single-mechanism systems.
