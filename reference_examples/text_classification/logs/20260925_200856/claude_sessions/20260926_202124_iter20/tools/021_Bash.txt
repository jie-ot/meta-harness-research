# Iteration 18 Report

## What changed
Two new memory-content / LLM-in-learning systems on top of decayed_confusion_memory.

- **reasoning_trace_memory**: stored the LLM's reasoning chain alongside each correct example
  and injected a truncated reasoning prefix for the top-K most similar examples at predict time.
- **error_summary_memory**: after batches with ≥5 errors, called the LLM once to synthesize a
  2-3 sentence narrative "Current weaknesses" hint and injected it above the few-shot examples.

## Per-dataset results vs frontier (decayed_confusion 52.2%)

| system | USPTO | LawBench | Symptom2Disease | avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| decayed_confusion_memory (frontier) | 26.7% | 40.0% | 90.0% | 52.2% |
| reasoning_trace_memory | 23.3% | 36.0% | 86.0% | 48.4% (-3.8) |
| error_summary_memory | 26.7% | 32.0% | 88.0% | 48.9% (-3.3) |

Both regressed. Symptom2Disease dropped 2-4 pp; LawBench dropped 4-8 pp.

## Why they regressed

**reasoning_trace_memory**: Reasoning chains are highly instance-specific and verbose. Injecting
reasoning prefixes from retrieved (but different) examples creates misleading procedural noise.
The model follows the injected reasoning pattern rather than reasoning freshly, which hurts on
different-but-similar queries. Also consumes significant token budget.

**error_summary_memory**: The synthesized narrative hint was too abstract to provide actionable
signal. It described broad error patterns but the model already had those patterns implicitly in
its few-shot examples. Worse, the hint consumed ~300-500 chars of the context budget, displacing
actual examples, causing LawBench (large label space, limited examples per label) to regress most.

## Takeaway

LLM-generated content injected at predict time (whether reasoning traces or narrative hints) has
consistently underperformed since iter 7 (llm_rule_synthesis 44.0%). The extra inference content
displaces concrete examples that are more reliably useful. Axis B (memory content enrichment) and
axis F (LLM usage in learning) have both been exhausted for now. Future iterations should focus on
selection algorithm (C) or learning trigger (E) changes, or entirely novel retrieval architectures.
